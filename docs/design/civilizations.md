# Civilizations — Calima: Flames of the Atlantic

Eight civilizations divided into three historical layers: Ancient, Native, and European Invaders.
Each civ has 1 hero unit (unique, max 1 per match), 1 unique military unit, and passive bonuses.

## Design Rules

- Each civ has exactly 1 hero unit (spawns near Town Center at game start, irrecoverable if lost)
- Each civ has 1 unique military unit (unlocked at Castle Age)
- Each civ has 2–3 passive bonuses
- Bonuses must not overlap with another civ's core identity
- Missing unit types must be compensated by bonuses elsewhere

---

## Layer I — Ancient Civilizations

### Atlantes
*Masters of the ocean. Builders of things men should not yet have built.*

- **Lore**: Inspired by the Atlantis myth — classical Greek writers (Plato, Pliny) placed the lost continent in the Atlantic, near the Pillars of Hercules. In the game, the Atlantes are a declining but technologically superior seafaring civilization with ruins scattered across the archipelago.
- **Playstyle**: Naval dominance, coastal expansion, water construction
- **Bonuses**:
  - Extended line of sight on coastal and water tiles
  - Can build floating platforms on water (acts as land for buildings)
  - Amphibious units — military units pay no movement penalty crossing shallow water
- **Unique Unit**: *Tidecaller* — ranged naval infantry, effective vs. buildings near water
- **Hero**: **Artaxerax** — *Calima* ability: generates an artificial fog-of-war cloud in a radius, hiding own units for 12s
- **Missing**: Heavy cavalry, siege workshop

---

### Fenicios
*They were here first. Before the conquest, before the natives — they drew the maps.*

- **Lore**: Phoenician and Carthaginian expeditions to the "Fortunate Islands" (6th–3rd century BC) are documented by Hanno the Navigator and referenced by Plutarch and Pliny. They left no permanent settlements but left knowledge — and legend.
- **Playstyle**: Commercial economy, trade networks, mercenary army
- **Bonuses**:
  - Market available from Dark Age (no age requirement)
  - Can recruit units from other civilizations' rosters at the Market (mercenaries, at gold cost)
  - Merchant ships passively generate gold while idle at a dock
- **Unique Unit**: *Trireme* — fast war galley with superior ramming damage
- **Hero**: **Hannón el Navegante** — *Trade Route* ability: activates a passive gold corridor between two map points for 30s
- **Missing**: Knights, castle-tier infantry

---

## Layer II — Native Civilizations

### Guanches
*They did not come to these islands. They were born from them.*

- **Lore**: The Guanches of Tenerife were the last native group to resist the Castilian conquest. The Battle of Acentejo (1494) was a decisive native victory led by Bencomo, Mencey of Taoro. They lived on the slopes of the Teide volcano, which they called *Echeyde* — the gateway to the underworld.
- **Playstyle**: Strong defense on volcanic terrain, durable infantry, late-game fortresses
- **Bonuses**:
  - No movement penalty on malpaís (volcanic rock) and lava-cooled tiles
  - Stone buildings +20% HP
  - Start with Spearmen available (no Barracks age requirement)
- **Unique Unit**: *Mencey's Guard* — heavy infantry with high armor, bonus damage vs. cavalry
- **Hero**: **Bencomo** — *Mencey's Charge* ability: nearby units gain +30% attack speed for 10s
- **Missing**: Cavalry, gunpowder units

---

### Canarii
*They built no walls because they needed none. Everyone knew where the line was.*

- **Lore**: The Canarii of Gran Canaria were the most politically organized native civilization, led by a council of chiefs (*guaires*). Doramas, their champion, was legendary for his individual combat prowess. The island's geography — ravines, high plateaus, coastal cliffs — was their natural fortress.
- **Playstyle**: Balanced economy, high villager output, cheap archery
- **Bonuses**:
  - Villagers gather Food +15% faster
  - Houses also function as secondary drop-off points for Food
  - Archers cost -20% food
- **Unique Unit**: *Ravine Archer* — light archer with bonus damage on elevated terrain (risco tiles)
- **Hero**: **Doramas** — *Challenge* ability: forces a single enemy unit to target only him for 8s, separating it from its group
- **Missing**: Heavy cavalry, full siege tree

---

### Mahos
*The desert teaches patience. And patience is a weapon.*

- **Lore**: The Mahos inhabited Lanzarote and Fuerteventura — the driest, most barren islands. Their king Guadarfía navigated a complex double game between his own people and Jean de Béthencourt during the French conquest of 1402, using diplomacy where others used spears.
- **Playstyle**: Fast light units, arid terrain mastery, wood-light building
- **Bonuses**:
  - Buildings cost -30% Wood (constructed with dry stone)
  - Scouts and light cavalry +25% movement speed
  - Extended line of sight on dune and desert tiles
- **Unique Unit**: *Sand Raider* — fast light cavalry, effective on dune terrain, bonus speed when not in combat
- **Hero**: **Guadarfía** — *Ambush* ability: becomes invisible on dune/desert tiles until attacking or taking damage
- **Missing**: Full archery upgrades, naval strength

## Layer III — European Invaders

### Francos
*They came with contracts, not armies. At first.*

- **Lore**: Jean de Béthencourt, a Norman lord in service of the Castilian crown, arrived in 1402 and conquered Lanzarote and Fuerteventura — the first European foothold in the archipelago. He exploited the Mahos' internal divisions, allying with Guadarfía. His conquest was administrative and systematic.
- **Playstyle**: Fast age advancement, organized cavalry, early expansion pressure
- **Bonuses**:
  - Age advancement costs -15% resources
  - Cavalry +15% HP
  - Farms are built 20% faster
- **Unique Unit**: *Chevalier Normand* — medium cavalry with bonus damage vs. native infantry
- **Hero**: **Jean de Béthencourt** — *Forced Diplomacy* ability: temporarily converts a nearby native unit to fight for you for 12s
- **Missing**: Full archery, strong late-game naval

---

### Britanos
*They never came to stay. They came to take and leave.*

- **Lore**: English privateers, pirates, and traders operated in the Canarian waters throughout the 15th–17th centuries. Francis Drake sacked Las Palmas de Gran Canaria in 1595, though he was ultimately repelled. The English interest was commercial and predatory — not colonization, but disruption.
- **Playstyle**: Naval supremacy, long-range archers, resource extraction via raiding
- **Bonuses**:
  - Foot archers +1 range per Age (Feudal onwards)
  - Warships attack +20% faster
  - Units that kill enemies in a radius around the hero generate +gold
- **Unique Unit**: *Longbowman* — foot archer with exceptional range, outranges most defenses
- **Hero**: **Francis Drake** — *Plunder* ability: every unit killed near him drops +gold for 20s
- **Missing**: Heavy cavalry upgrades, strong defensive buildings

---

### Castellanos
*Late to arrive. Impossible to remove.*

- **Lore**: The Crown of Castile funded the final conquest of the archipelago. Alonso Fernández de Lugo conquered Tenerife in 1496 after two campaigns — the first ending in the catastrophic native victory at Acentejo. He founded La Laguna (still the historic capital of Tenerife) and completed the pacification of all seven islands.
- **Playstyle**: Powerful in Castle and Imperial Age, heavy infantry, superior late technology
- **Bonuses**:
  - Advancing to a new Age unlocks one free technology in the Blacksmith
  - Swordsmen line +15% HP
  - Towers and Castles have +10% range
- **Unique Unit**: *Conquistador* — armored infantry-cavalry hybrid, effective vs. all native unit types
- **Hero**: **Don Quijote de la Mancha** — *Knight Errant's Charge* ability: charges in a straight line toward a target point, damaging everything in the path (friend or foe). Passive *Rocinante*: +15% movement speed but attacks with a slight delay. High risk, high reward.
- **Missing**: Fast early-game; economy is slow to start

---

## Hero Rules (all civilizations)

- **One per match**: only one hero exists per player; if killed, they are gone permanently
- **Spawns at game start**: near the Town Center, at Dark Age
- **Cannot be trained**: they are not in any queue; loss is final
- **Stronger than standard units**: roughly equivalent to a Castle-Age unique unit in stats
- **Ability cooldown**: each hero ability has a cooldown of 45–60 seconds
