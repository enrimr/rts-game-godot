Overview

Each of the 8 civilizations in Calima has two legendary heroes: one male and one female. At the start of each match, one is randomly selected to lead your forces. Both share their civilization's identity but bring unique tactical abilities to the battlefield.

GUANCHES (Tenerife)

Bencomo — Mencey of Taoro

Historical Context: Bencomo was the most powerful mencey (king) of Tenerife during the Spanish conquest. He led the Guanche victory at the First Battle of Acentejo in 1494, where indigenous forces devastated the Spanish army in a narrow ravine. His tactical genius and knowledge of the volcanic terrain made him a legendary figure of resistance.

Ability: Mencey's Charge — Rallies nearby allied units, granting them +30% attack speed for 10s.

Tactical Role: Offensive buffer — turns average armies into devastating war machines during critical engagements.

Dácil — Queen of the Valley

Historical Context: Dácil (also known as Catalina) was a Guanche princess from Tenerife. According to legend, she was captured by Spanish conquistadors but earned her freedom through her dignity and wisdom. Some chronicles describe her as a peacemaker who tried to prevent unnecessary bloodshed, while others portray her as a symbol of resilience who never abandoned her people's cause.

Ability: Mountain Voice — Grants nearby allies +2 armor (melee and pierce) and +50% healing rate for 12s.

Tactical Role: Defensive sustain — holds defensive lines and enables protracted battles.

CANARII (Gran Canaria)

Doramas — Guardian of Tamar

Historical Context: Doramas was a legendary Canarii warrior who fought the Spanish conquest of Gran Canaria in the 1480s. Known for his unmatched combat prowess, he is said to have killed multiple Spanish soldiers in single combat. He was finally defeated at the Battle of Ajódar in 1485, where he fought to the death rather than surrender. His bravery became a symbol of Canarii resistance.

Ability: Challenge — Forces one enemy to target only this hero for 8s (taunt).

Tactical Role: Frontline tank — absorbs damage while allies flank or escape.

Guayarmina — Guardian of Tara

Historical Context: Guayarmina was a princess of Gran Canaria and, according to Canarii oral tradition, one of the finest archers ever seen on the island. Chronicles describe her as defending sacred mountain sites from invaders with uncanny accuracy. Some versions of the legend say she could pierce a sparrow's eye at 200 paces. She represents the Canarii ideal of precision, focus, and deadly effectiveness.

Ability: Fate's Arrow — Fires an unblockable arrow dealing 80 damage (ignores armor). Cooldown is halved if the target dies.

Tactical Role: Sniper assassin — eliminates priority targets (enemy heroes, siege weapons).

MAHOS (Fuerteventura)

Guadarfía — Desert Shadow

Historical Context: Guadarfía was a chieftain of the Mahos people during the Norman conquest of Fuerteventura. Known for guerrilla tactics and ambush warfare, he used the island's barren terrain to his advantage, striking Spanish patrols and vanishing into the dunes. His name became synonymous with cunning and unpredictability, traits that made him a nightmare for occupying forces.

Ability: Ambush — Becomes invisible until attacking or taking damage (15s max).

Tactical Role: Stealth striker — infiltrates enemy lines, scouts, or assassinates key units.

Tibiabin — Rider of the Dunes

Historical Context: Tibiabin was a legendary queen of the Mahos people, remembered in oral tradition as a master of desert survival and spiritual communion with the land. She is said to have commanded sandstorms and used them to blind invaders. Whether literal or metaphorical, her legend embodies the Mahos belief that the desert itself can be weaponized by those who understand its rhythms.

Ability: Sandstorm — Creates a 200px sandstorm for 10s: enemies inside take 3 damage/s, -40% movement speed, and -50% projectile accuracy.

Tactical Role: Area denial — blocks choke points, disrupts enemy formations, protects retreats.

FRANKS (Norman Conquerors)

Jean de Béthencourt — Norman Conqueror

Historical Context: Jean de Béthencourt was a Norman nobleman who led the first successful European colonization of the Canary Islands in 1402. Known for his diplomatic skills, he often brokered alliances with local chiefs rather than relying solely on force. His strategy of "forced diplomacy" — converting enemies into allies through political maneuvering — allowed him to control Lanzarote and Fuerteventura with minimal bloodshed.

Ability: Forced Diplomacy — Temporarily converts a nearby native unit for 12s.

Tactical Role: Utility converter — turns enemy strength against them in critical moments.

Catalina de Béthencourt — Countess Conqueror

Historical Context: Catalina de Béthencourt is a fictionalized character inspired by Norman noblewomen who accompanied their husbands on conquests and sometimes fought alongside them. The historical Béthencourt expedition included women who took up arms when besieged. Catalina represents the code of knightly honor — single combat, duels of prowess, and the belief that leadership is proven through personal courage rather than command from afar.

Ability: Honor Duel — Challenges an enemy hero or unique unit: both deal +100% damage to each other and -50% to others for 15s. If Catalina wins, she heals 50% max HP.

Tactical Role: Hero hunter — eliminates enemy leaders in decisive 1v1 combat.

BRITONS (English Privateers)

Francis Drake — The Dragon

Historical Context: Sir Francis Drake was an English privateer and naval commander who terrorized Spanish shipping in the Atlantic during the 16th century. Known as "El Draque" (The Dragon) by the Spanish, he circumnavigated the globe and brought immense wealth back to England through plunder. His raids on Spanish treasure fleets made him a hero in England and a pirate in Spain. He died off the coast of Panama in 1596.

Ability: Plunder — Kills near the hero yield bonus gold for 20s.

Tactical Role: Economic accelerator — profits from battles while fighting.

Grace O'Malley — Pirate Queen

Historical Context: Grace O'Malley (Gráinne Mhaol) was an Irish pirate queen who commanded a fleet in the 16th century. She defied English authority, led naval raids, and negotiated directly with Queen Elizabeth I on equal terms. Known for her audacious boarding actions — leaping onto enemy ships with sword in hand — she became a symbol of Irish resistance and female autonomy. Her legend rivals Drake's but is less known outside Ireland.

Ability: Boarding Action — Dashes 200px in a line, dealing 30 damage and stunning enemies for 2s. Deals 100 damage to buildings.

Tactical Role: Initiator — breaks enemy formations, interrupts siege weapons, disrupts ranged units.

CASTELLANOS (Castilian Conquistadors)

Don Quijote — Knight Errant

Historical Context: Don Quijote is the fictional protagonist of Miguel de Cervantes' 1605 novel, but his archetype — the idealistic knight fighting impossible odds — reflects the spirit of Spanish conquistadors who believed they were on righteous crusades. Quijote's delusions of grandeur, charging at windmills thinking them giants, mirror the sometimes absurd overconfidence of conquest-era Spain. Yet his unwavering honor and courage embody the Castilian self-image.

Ability: Knight Errant Charge — Charges in a straight line, damaging everything in the path.

Tactical Role: Dive assassin — splits enemy backlines, kills ranged units, disrupts formations.

Dulcinea del Toboso — Lady of Strategy

Historical Context: Dulcinea del Toboso is Don Quijote's idealized love interest in Cervantes' novel — a simple peasant woman transformed in his mind into a noble lady. In Calima, we reimagine her as the reality behind the ideal: not a passive muse but an active leader who inspired others to fight. She represents the gap between romantic fantasy and practical necessity — Quijote charged windmills, but someone had to hold the line.

Ability: Call to Arms — Summons 3 temporary Militia (+20% HP and attack) for 40s. They don't count as casualties when lost.

Tactical Role: Force multiplier — reinforces desperate defenses or adds mass to attacks.

ATLANTES (Mythical Sea People)

Artaxerax — Sea King

Historical Context: Artaxerax is a fictional Atlantean king inspired by Plato's dialogues Timaeus and Critias. In those texts, Atlantis was a naval power that controlled much of the Atlantic before sinking beneath the waves. Artaxerax's ability to summon artificial fog reflects Atlantis's mythical mastery over ocean weather and their reputation as a civilization shrouded in mystery.

Ability: Calima — Creates an artificial fog patch hiding own units for 12s.

Tactical Role: Stealth enabler — hides entire armies for ambushes or retreats.

Cleito — Mistress of Tides

Historical Context: Cleito was, according to Plato, the mortal wife of Poseidon and the mother of the first Atlantean kings. In myth, Poseidon surrounded her hill with rings of water to protect her, creating the circular geography of Atlantis. In Calima, she represents the healing and life-giving power of the ocean — the flip side of its destructive storms. Where Artaxerax hides, Cleito sustains.

Ability: Rising Tide — Creates a 250px wave: allies heal 60 HP and gain +30% movement speed for 8s; enemies take 20 damage and -20% speed for 4s.

Tactical Role: Hybrid support — heals after battles, enables tactical repositioning, disrupts enemy advances.

FENICIOS (Phoenician Traders)

Hannón — Master Navigator

Historical Context: Hanno the Navigator was a Carthaginian explorer who led an expedition down the west coast of Africa around 500 BCE. His Periplus (voyage account) describes encounters with gorillas, volcanic eruptions, and coastal trade. He represents Phoenician mercantile expansion — the belief that wealth comes from controlling trade routes rather than conquering territory.

Ability: Trade Route — Generates passive gold for 30s (8 gold/second).

Tactical Role: Economic passive — funds expensive unit production without risking villagers.

Elissa — Founder of Carthage

Historical Context: Elissa (also known as Dido) was the legendary founder of Carthage around 814 BCE. According to Virgil's Aeneid, she fled Phoenicia after her brother murdered her husband, then tricked a local Berber king into granting her as much land as an ox hide could encompass. She cut the hide into thin strips and encircled an entire hill, securing the site of Carthage through wit rather than war. Her cunning embodies the Phoenician spirit of leveraging resources cleverly.

Ability: Mercenary Pact — Spends 400 gold to permanently convert one enemy unit (non-hero, non-building) within 200px.

Tactical Role: Tactical swing — steals expensive units (Knights, Trebuchets, unique units) for instant army composition advantage.

Design Philosophy

Each civilization's hero pair represents a tactical duality: - Offensive vs Defensive (Bencomo vs Dácil) - Tank vs Sniper (Doramas vs Guayarmina) - Stealth vs Control (Guadarfía vs Tibiabin) - Utility vs Duelist (Jean vs Catalina) - Economic vs Combat (Drake vs Grace) - Dive vs Summon (Quijote vs Dulcinea) - Stealth vs Healing (Artaxerax vs Cleito) - Passive vs Active (Hannón vs Elissa)

This ensures no civilization has a "correct" hero choice — both are viable, and the random selection at match start adds strategic variety and replayability.

Gameplay Impact

  • 16 unique heroes total (8 male + 8 female)
  • Random 50/50 selection at match start
  • All heroes respawn at Town Center in standard matches
  • Regicide mode: hero death = instant defeat
  • Heroes are free (0 population cost, 0 resource cost)
  • Abilities have cooldowns (50-120 seconds)
