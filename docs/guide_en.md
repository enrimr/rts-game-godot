# Calima: Flames of the Atlantic — Game Manual

---

## 1. Welcome to the archipelago

The eastern Atlantic Ocean, a hundred kilometres off the African coast: the Canary Islands. Seven islands of volcanoes, laurel forests, dunes and black-sand beaches. To their native peoples, home. To the empires of the Mediterranean and Europe, a gateway: the last landfall before the open ocean.

*Calima* is a real-time strategy game in which eight civilizations — ancient, native and European — fight for control of the archipelago. You gather four resources, raise a settlement, advance through four Ages, research technologies and command armies on land and at sea. The game takes its name from the calima: the Saharan wind that wraps the islands in dust and silence — and can decide a battle here.

This manual covers the whole game. If this is your first match, start with chapter 2 and keep chapter 4 (controls) at hand; the rest is reference material.

---

## 2. Getting started: your first match

A guide to the first ten minutes of any skirmish.

1. **Create the match.** From the main menu choose Skirmish, your civilization (the Franks are a straightforward start: strong cavalry, cheap Ages), a Plains map and one rival. Start the game.
2. **Take stock.** You begin with a Town Center, a few villagers, a Scout, your hero, and 200 Food, 75 Wood and 50 Gold. Your starting population cap is 15.
3. **Put the villagers to work.** Select them and right-click berry bushes or a nearby animal. Queue more villagers at the Town Center (key **V**): economy rules everything.
4. **Scout.** Select the Scout and toggle its auto-explore, or steer it with right-clicks. Find gold, stone, forests, wild herds — and your enemy.
5. **Wood and houses.** Send two or three villagers to chop trees. At 25 Wood, build a House (**B** opens the build menu, then **H**): each one raises the population cap by 5.
6. **Drop-off points.** Villagers earn by the trip. Build a Lumber Camp (**L**) beside the forest and a Mill (**M**) near your food.
7. **First army.** A Barracks (**B**) trains Militia. Half a dozen deter an early raid.
8. **Advance an Age.** At 500 Food, press the advance button on the Town Center. The Feudal Age opens archers, cavalry, the Blacksmith and the Market.
9. **React to notifications.** Toasts carry shortcut buttons: one click places a House after a pop-cap warning, **Space** jumps to the last attack alert.
10. **Win.** In the default mode (Conquest) you eliminate a rival by destroying their units and buildings. The rest of this manual gives you the tools.

## 3. The interface

- **Top bar** — your Food, Wood, Gold and Stone stockpiles (with gatherer counts), current/maximum population and your Age.
- **Bottom-left panel** — the current selection: portraits (up to 40 units), the first unit's name, health and stats, and the selected building's training queue and active research.
- **Action grid** — the selection's contextual buttons: train, build, research, stances, formations. Every button shows its hotkey on a badge and its real cost in a tooltip.
- **Minimap** (bottom right) — terrain, resources, and every known unit and building. Right-click it to issue move orders; a docked button opens the **players panel** (each player's colour, name, civilization, Age and live score).
- **Bottom bar** — game-speed buttons (single-player), idle-villager / idle-military / locate-hero cycle buttons, an optional camera d-pad.
- **Notifications** — attacks, buildings finished or lost, population cap, hero in danger. Every toast carries a direct-action button and is clickable to jump to the spot.
- **Control-group chips** — clickable chips showing each assigned group's composition; double-click centres the camera.
- **Weather banner** — announces the forecast and counts down the active event.

## 5. Economy

### The four resources

| Resource | Sources | Drop-off |
|---|---|---|
| **Food** | Berries, hunting, sheep, Farms, fishing | Mill (the Town Center too) |
| **Wood** | Trees | Lumber Camp |
| **Gold** | Gold deposits; Market trading | Mining Camp |
| **Stone** | Stone quarries | Mining Camp |

You start with 200 Food, 75 Wood, 50 Gold and 0 Stone (Normal mode; Scarce and Abundant scale the map's deposits, Full Combat sets all four stockpiles to 9999).

Villagers carry up to **10 resource units** per trip: distance to the drop-off is your economy's real cost. Two Blacksmith technologies raise everyone's carry capacity (+25 % each), and each camp's own tech line adds +15 % gather speed and +10 % carry per step for its resource (chapter 9).

### Herding: the Presa Canario

The Mill trains the **Presa Canario** (30 Food + 10 Gold, Dark Age), a herding dog. Select it and right-click any animal: the dog fetches it, takes it in tow and leads it to your nearest drop-off. Along the way:

- **Enemy and wild sheep convert** to your side while herded.
- Every completed trip **pays food** in proportion to how far the dog brought the animal home.
- **Wild flocks** graze across the map, away from every base — the AI raises its own dogs, so unclaimed herds are a race.
- The dog bites when attacked (it is a guard dog), but **never abandons a herding trip on its own**; only an explicit order from you makes it drop the flock.

Once the animal is home, a villager slaughters it (right-click with any selection that isn't dogs-only) and gathers it as game.

### Farms and fishing

- The **Farm** (60 Wood) is continuous food worked by one villager; when it runs out, right-click to replant it.
- On water maps, **Fishing Boats** pull food from fish shoals and deliver it at the Dock; they also build **Fish Traps** (75 Wood), which generate passive food.

### Population

You start with a cap of **15**; each **House** (25 Wood) adds **+5** on completion. At the cap, training halts: the HUD flashes and a notification offers to place a House in one click.

### The Market

From the Feudal Age (Phoenicians: from the Dark Age), the Market sells Food, Wood or Stone for Gold and buys them back. Rates are **per player** and degrade with use, recovering over time — dumping 500 wood at once worsens your own price. Every civilization can also **hire mercenaries**: units from other civilizations paid in gold, on a per-unit cooldown (Phoenicians pay 25 % less).

## 6. The Ages

You advance an Age at the Town Center whenever you can pay. Each Age unlocks units, buildings and technologies.

| Age | Cost | Time | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dark** | starting age | — | Villager, Militia, Scout, Presa Canario, basic buildings, Dock, Fishing Boat |
| **Feudal** | 500 Food | ~2 min 10 s | Archer, Heavy Scout, Archery Range, Blacksmith, Stable, Market, Watch Tower, War Galley, Transport Ship, Trireme, Sand Raider |
| **Castle** | 800 Food + 200 Gold | ~2 min 40 s | Pikeman, Knight, University, Temple, Siege Workshop, additional Town Centers, most unique units, Harimaguada |
| **Imperial** | 1000 Food + 800 Gold | ~3 min 10 s | Wonder, Trebuchet, the top of every tech line |

The **Franks** advance 15 % cheaper; the **Castilians** get a free Blacksmith technology with every advance; the **Guanches** field Pikemen from the Dark Age.

## 10. Combat

### The counter triangle

Every attack carries a flat bonus against certain target classes — army composition matters as much as army size:

| Attacker | Bonus | Against |
|---|---|---|
| Pikeman | +12 | Cavalry |
| Knight, Heavy Scout, Sand Raider, Chevalier Normand | +4 | Archers |
| Scout | +3 | Archers |
| Archer, Ravine Archer, Conquistador | +3 | Spearmen |
| Longbowman | +4 / +3 | Cavalry / Spearmen |

**Pikes punish cavalry, cavalry eats archers, archers cut down spearmen.** The AI reads your composition and trains counters: single-unit spam loses to a mixed army.

Base damage is *attack − armour* (melee or pierce, by weapon), with a floor of 1.

### Stances

Four stances per military unit (panel buttons). They govern what the unit does **on its own**; an explicit attack order always chases:

- **Aggressive** (default) — chases any enemy it detects
- **Defensive** — fights back on a short leash and returns to its post
- **Stand Ground** — strikes whatever it can reach without moving
- **No Attack** — never fights on its own

Idle units spot hostiles within sight (~240 px) and react according to their stance.

### Formations

Group moves use the chosen formation: **Line** (melee front, ranged behind, siege at the rear), **Box**, **Spread** (against area damage) or **Rings**. Picking one reforms the selection in place.

### Garrison

- Town Center: 10 slots; Watch Tower: 5; Transport Ship: 8.
- Every garrisoned soldier **adds an arrow** to the building's volley (the Town Center only fires while garrisoned).
- Right-clicking your Town Center or tower garrisons **military**; villagers use their Garrison button or the Town Bell.
- The garrison dies if the building falls.

### Healing

The **Harimaguada** heals 5 HP/s at close range and auto-triages the most wounded ally around her. The **Temple** heals its garrison at 4 HP/s (heroes at half rate). Units do not regenerate on their own.

### Siege and special orders

- The **Battering Ram** demolishes buildings and barely scratches units. The **Mangonel** deals area damage but cannot fire point-blank. The **Trebuchet** outranges everything: it deploys to fire and packs up to move.
- Ranged and siege units can **attack ground** (bombard a spot with no target) and use **cover fire** (**C**): walk into range, then open fire.

## 12. Weather

Weather events arrive procedurally (lobby frequency: Off / Normal / Frequent / Extreme). A **forecast** warns you seconds ahead; the HUD banner counts the event down. Effects ramp in and out gradually.

| Weather | Effects |
|---|---|
| **Calima** | Vision −40 %, food and wood gathering −20 %, land speed −15 % |
| **Atlantic Storm** | Naval speed −30 %, fishing −50 %, arrows drift with the crosswind |
| **Sea Fog** | Coastal vision −60 %; units near the coast are **cloaked**: spotted only at close range (180 px; 90 px against Atlanteans) or for 3 s after they attack |
| **Trade Winds** | Ships ±20 % speed by heading; projectiles drift |
| **Volcanic Ash** | Only around calderas (Volcanic Coast): vision −50 %, gathering −30 %, buildings take damage over time |

Every civilization shrugs off some weather (chapter 14): the Guanches ignore the Calima, the Atlanteans the Atlantic Storm, and so on.

## 13. Naval and amphibious warfare

On **Islands** maps the sea is the main theatre; the Volcanic and Desert coasts are contested too. Everything starts with a **Dock** (150 Wood) on the shoreline.

| Unit | Age | Cost | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fishing Boat** | Dark | 75 Wood | Fishes the shoals and delivers at the Dock; builds Fish Traps |
| **Transport Ship** | Feudal | 125 Wood | Carries 8 land units (villagers included); unloads on dry land |
| **War Galley** | Feudal | 75 Wood + 35 Gold | Ranged warship |
| **Trireme** (Phoenicians) | Feudal | 100 Wood + 50 Gold | Ram: ×2 vs ships plus knockback; gold trickle |

- New ships appear at the Dock's **berth** in open water; fishing boats return there to unload.
- Load an army and right-click the far shore: the transport crosses and unloads by itself. An island expansion needs villagers aboard.
- The Atlantean **Tidecaller** is the only land unit that enters the sea: it wades shallows at full speed and swims open water at 60 %.
- The AI also fishes, patrols with galleys, burns your coastline (docks and traps first) and lands assaults.

## 14. Civilizations

Eight civilizations in three historical layers. Your choice sets your bonuses, your unique unit and your hero pair.

### Ancient civilizations

#### Atlanteans — *"The sea is not a barrier. It is a road."*

The fading empire of myth, with the world's finest seamanship. They own the water: no shoreline stops an Atlantean attack.

**Bonuses**

- +50 % vision within 400 px of a coast
- Harder to spot in Sea Fog (found at 90 px, not 180)
- Ship attack speed ×1.20; immune to the Atlantic Storm

**Unique unit — Tidecaller** (Barracks, Castle Age, 50 Food + 70 Gold): amphibious infantry with an area Tidal Pulse; the only land unit that swims.

**Hero — Artaxerax:** *Calima* — veils nearby allies for 12 s; while it lasts, they cannot be targeted.

**Heroine — Cleito:** *Rising Tide* — a 250 px wave that heals allies 60 HP and hastens them +30 % for 8 s; enemies take 20 damage and lose 20 % speed.

**Weaknesses:** no heavy cavalry, no siege workshop — they struggle on pure land maps.

#### Phoenicians — *"We need not conquer what we can simply buy."*

The mapmakers of the Atlantic. They live off the Market, open from the Dark Age.

**Bonuses**

- Market from the Dark Age; mercenaries −25 %
- Ships −15 % cost; Triremes trickle passive gold (5 per 30 s)
- Half the Atlantic Storm penalty

**Unique unit — Trireme** (Dock, Feudal Age, 100 Wood + 50 Gold): ram — ×2 vs ships, with knockback.

**Hero — Hanno the Navigator:** *Trade Route* — establishes a route that yields 50 gold over 30 s.

**Heroine — Elissa:** *Mercenary Pact* — 400 gold permanently converts a nearby enemy unit.

**Weaknesses:** vulnerable to early aggression; no knights.

### Native civilizations

#### Guanches — *"Echeyde is not a mountain. It is a gate. And it is ours."*

The kingdoms of Tenerife, masters of volcanic ground.

**Bonuses**

- Cross **malpaís** freely; stone buildings +20 % HP
- Pikemen from the **Dark Age** — the earliest anti-cavalry in the game
- Immune to the Calima; half the Volcanic Ash penalty

**Unique unit — Menceyes Guard** (Barracks, Castle Age, 65 Food + 25 Gold): heavy infantry that enters a Rage Aura below half health.

**Hero — Bencomo:** *Mencey's Rush* — +30 % attack speed for nearby allies for 10 s.

**Heroine — Dácil:** *Voice of the Mountain* — +2 armour and +50 % healing speed for 12 s.

**Weaknesses:** no cavalry; weak at sea.

#### Canarii — *"The ravine is not a problem. It is a wall that builds itself."*

Gran Canaria's economic powerhouse — and the protagonists of the campaign.

**Bonuses**

- Villagers +15 % gathering Food; **Houses double as drop-off points**
- Archers −20 % Food cost
- Half the Sea Fog and Trade Winds penalties

**Unique unit — Ravine Archer** (Archery Range, Castle Age, 40 Wood + 55 Gold): Ambush Shot — the first shot fired standing still deals ×2; gains range on riscos.

**Hero — Doramas:** *Challenge* — taunts the nearest enemy unit into attacking only him for 6 s.

**Heroine — Guayarmina:** *Arrow of Fate* — 80 damage ignoring armour; if the target dies, the cooldown is halved.

**Weaknesses:** no naval strength; they struggle on flat, open maps.

#### Mahos — *"The desert does not kill you. Your hurry does."*

The dry-stone builders of Lanzarote and Fuerteventura. Fast, relentless harassment.

**Bonuses**

- Buildings −30 % Wood cost
- Scouts and light cavalry +25 % speed
- Cross **dunes** freely and see +40 % from them; half the Calima penalty

**Unique unit — Sand Raider** (Stable, Feudal Age, 60 Food + 40 Gold): hit and run — retreats automatically after every attack.

**Hero — Guadarfía:** *Ambush* — near-invisible for 8 s; enemies cannot auto-attack him.

**Heroine — Tibiabin:** *Sandstorm* — a 200 px storm for 10 s: 3 damage per second, −40 % speed and −50 % projectile accuracy for enemies inside.

**Weaknesses:** weak late game; poor archery and navy.

### European invaders

#### Franks — *"The contract was signed. The islands were already ours on paper."*

Jean de Béthencourt's Normans: early expansion and timing attacks.

**Bonuses**

- Age advance −15 % cost
- Cavalry +15 % HP
- Farms built faster

**Unique unit — Chevalier Normand** (Stable, Castle Age, 75 Food + 65 Gold): Lance Charge — ×2.5 damage after 80 px of uninterrupted approach.

**Hero — Jean de Béthencourt:** *Forced Diplomacy* — converts the nearest enemy unit to your side for 12 s.

**Heroine — Catalina de Béthencourt:** *Duel of Honour* — a 15 s single combat: +100 % mutual damage and −50 % against everyone else; victory restores half her health.

**Weaknesses:** weak archery; limited navy.

#### Britons — *"We haven't come to stay. We've come to leave with more than we brought."*

Privateers and archers. The best warships and the longest reach in the game.

**Bonuses**

- Foot archers +1 range per Age (from Feudal)
- Warships +20 % attack speed
- Half the Atlantic Storm penalty

**Unique unit — Longbowman** (Archery Range, Castle Age, 30 Wood + 60 Gold): exceptional range and armour-piercing arrows (+4 vs cavalry).

**Hero — Francis Drake:** *Plunder* — for 20 s, every enemy felled in his radius pays 15 gold.

**Heroine — Grace O'Malley:** *Boarding* — a 200 px dash dealing 30 damage plus a 2 s stun (100 damage to buildings).

**Weaknesses:** weak heavy cavalry; light defenses.

#### Castilians — *"We are not the first. We will be the last."*

The crown that finished the conquest. Slow to start, unstoppable late.

**Bonuses**

- One **free** Blacksmith technology with every Age advance
- Swordsman line +15 % HP; towers +10 % range
- Half the Calima penalty

**Unique unit — Conquistador** (Barracks, Castle Age, 60 Food + 60 Gold): Salvo Fire — 3 rapid shots with a 12 s cooldown.

**Hero — Don Quixote de la Mancha:** *Errant Knight's Charge* — a straight-line gallop damaging everything in its path, friend or foe. His passive *Rocinante* grants extra speed at the price of a slight delay on his blows.

**Heroine — Dulcinea del Toboso:** *Call to Arms* — summons 3 temporary Militia (+20 % HP and attack) for 40 s.

**Weaknesses:** slow early economy; their power needs Castle or Imperial Age.

## 15. Game modes and victory

### Skirmish setup

- Map type and size (Small / Medium / Large), starting resources (Scarce / Normal / Abundant / Full Combat)
- Your civilization and each rival's; 1 to 3 AI rivals
- **Teams 1–4** — 2v2, 2v1 or free-for-all; allied AIs share vision, come to your defense and announce their offensives
- Starting age, victory condition, weather frequency, hero gender
- AI difficulty is set in Settings (Easy / Normal / Hard)

### Maps

| Map | Description | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Plains** | Flat land, no special terrain | Ideal for beginners |
| **Standard** | Varied land terrain | General play |
| **Volcanic Coast** | Coastal strip with a caldera | Corridor fights; strong Guanches; Volcanic Ash |
| **Desert Coast** | Arid, wood-poor, ocean | Strong Mahos; wood discipline |
| **Islands** | Islands separated by ocean | Navy is mandatory |

Every map is procedurally generated each match.

### Victory conditions

- **Conquest** — eliminate every hostile side's units and buildings. No timers.
- **Regicide** — your hero is your life: their death is instant defeat, no respawn. Protect yours; hunt theirs.
- **Wonder** — build the Wonder in the Imperial Age; if it survives the **4-minute countdown**, you win. Destroying it cancels the clock and the match continues.

With teams, a side wins when only allies remain standing.

### Defeat and spectating

Surrendering (Esc → Surrender) or losing everything shows your defeat panel — but while hostile sides remain, the match plays on. **View map** lets you spectate the live battle (orders locked) until the final result.

## 17. Multiplayer

Up to **4 players** over LAN or the Internet, with teams and AI seats.

- **LAN** — one player hosts; the rest join using the address shown in the lobby header.
- **Internet** — the host presses the Internet button: the game opens the port via UPnP and shows the public IP (manual port forwarding as a fallback). There is also a **Steam** prototype (lobbies and friend invites).
- **The lobby** — every player picks a name, colour, civilization and team; the host configures the match, manages the remaining seats (Open / AI / Closed) and can kick. Chat in the lobby and, in match, with **Enter**.
- **Version check** — the host refuses clients running a different game version, with a clear message.
- **Reconnection** — if a player drops, their seat is held for 90 seconds while the match continues; on return they resume exactly where they were.
- **Save and resume** — the host can save an online match. On resume, the lobby reserves every original player's seat (the match may start with someone missing: their seat waits under the same reconnection grace).
- Pause belongs to the host. A player who surrenders or disconnects for good counts as resigned and may stay to spectate.

## 18. Replays

Every match records itself (toggle in Settings). The **Replays** browser on the main menu lets you watch, delete and export.

- **Playback** — pause, seek anywhere on the timeline (backwards too), change speed, and **reveal the whole map**. A replay is recorded history: it takes no orders.
- **Cinematic mode** (**C**) — hides the interface; optional floating minimap.
- **Video export** — renders the replay (or an **A/B**-marked clip) to a 30 FPS video file in the background, with no interface in the footage.
- The end-of-match screen offers **Watch replay** directly.

## 19. Saving, loading and settings

### Save and load

- **Esc → Save** at any moment: 99 slots with date, map and Age.
- Everything is preserved: units, buildings, in-progress research (already paid), garrisons, stances, the active weather, even the match's random sequence — loading does not reroll fate.
- Multiplayer matches are saved by the host and resumed from the lobby (chapter 17).

### Settings

- **Audio** — music and effects volume. All sound is synthesized: every civilization speaks its own invented language, with voices per unit class and gender.
- **Game** — language (English / Español), AI difficulty, replay recording.
- **Video** — fullscreen, VSync, FPS counter, and the **Unit style**: *Classic* (flat vector figures), *Enhanced* (outline, shading and extra animation) or *Redesigned* (lore-faithful remodelled figures). The change applies live to every unit on screen.
- **Controls** — remappable camera keys (arrows always work), edge scrolling, on-screen d-pad.
- **Videos** — whether to include the minimap in exported videos.

## 20. Know your enemy: the AI

- It plays your economy: it gathers, **builds with real villagers** (its construction sites can be raided), farms, herds with its own dogs, fishes and advances Ages. No resource cheats.
- **It is not omniscient**: it only attacks what it has seen. Buildings are remembered once scouted; units only while visible.
- It defends its base, rebuilds its Town Center if you destroy it, and escalates with its economy and the difficulty setting.
- It reads your army composition and trains the counters.
- On water it fishes, patrols, burns your coastline (docks and traps first) and lands assaults.
- AIs also fight **each other** — a free-for-all is a real multi-sided war.

## 21. Strategy tips

- **Villagers win games.** Produce villagers non-stop well into the match; a huge army over a dead economy is a one-battle army.
- **Scout early.** Gold, stone, wild herds, the enemy base: knowing where things are is your first advantage.
- **Shorten the trips.** Camp by the forest, Mill by the hunt. Every second of walking is resource lost.
- **Herd.** The Presa Canario earns food just for bringing sheep home, and every converted sheep is one the rival dog doesn't get.
- **Research as you advance.** An idle Blacksmith is a losing Blacksmith. Queue whole lines: they're paid up front and refunded on cancel.
- **Mix your army.** Pikes against cavalry, cavalry against archers, archers against pikes — and some siege for the walls.
- **Use the terrain.** Retreat across malpaís the rival cannot enter (Guanches), shoot from a risco (+2 range), hide your fleet in the Sea Fog.
- **Watch the sky.** The forecast warns you: don't launch your naval attack into an incoming Atlantic Storm.
- **In Regicide, the hero stays home.** And in Wonder mode, if the rival finishes theirs first you have exactly 4 minutes: drop everything.
- **In team games, communicate.** Allied AIs mark their targets and come when you're raided; a minimap ping (Alt + click) is worth a thousand words.

## 22. Sights and sounds

All of *Calima*'s art is vector-based and procedural, and all its audio is synthesized — there is not a single external texture or recording:

- Three unit styles (Classic / Enhanced / Redesigned), switchable live.
- Human units are randomly male or female (cosmetic; the Harimaguada is always female, by lore).
- Team colours on unit clothing and on building roofs, flags, banners and sails.
- Lore-faithful landmarks: the Temple is an almogarén — an open dry-stone Canarian shrine —, the Mill a stone tower with canvas sails.
- A living world: swell and coastal foam, pulsing caldera lava, trotting fauna, shadows, hero auras, progressive fire on damaged buildings.
- Every civilization speaks its own invented language, with its own accent and voices per unit class and gender.
