# Calima: Flames of the Atlantic — Player's Guide

---

## The World

The Canary Islands. Eastern Atlantic, 100 km off the coast of northwest Africa.

For the native inhabitants these were simply home — volcanic mountains, laurel forests, black sand beaches and desert plains across seven islands. For the Europeans who arrived in the 15th century they were a gateway: the last stop before the open ocean, the first prize of the Age of Exploration.

*Calima* puts you in command of one of eight civilizations fighting for control of this archipelago. The calima — the warm Saharan wind that blankets the islands in dust and silence — gives the game its name. War here is not fought on plains. It is fought on lava fields, clifftops, ocean straits, and dune crossings.

---

## Civilizations

### Who are they?

Eight civilizations drawn from three historical layers. Your choice determines your playstyle, your starting bonuses, and your unique hero.

---

### Ancient Civilizations

These civilizations predate the medieval conflict. Their ruins dot the archipelago. Playing them means being out of time — technologically different, strategically isolated, but capable of things no one else can do.

#### Atlantes
*"The sea is not a barrier. It is a road."*

The lost civilization of myth. Greek writers — Plato, Pliny, Herodotus — placed Atlantis beyond the Pillars of Hercules, in the very ocean these islands occupy. In *Calima*, the Atlantes are not a legend. They are a fading empire with the most advanced seafaring knowledge in the world, and ruins everywhere.

**How they play:** Atlantes control the water. They can build floating platforms on the ocean — the only civilization that can do this — turning sea tiles into buildable land. Their units cross shallow water without penalty. Their line of sight on coastal tiles is greater than any other civ.

**Strengths:** Naval dominance, coastal expansion, strategic map control  
**Weaknesses:** No heavy cavalry; siege is limited; loses badly on pure land maps

**Passive bonuses:**
- Extended vision on coastal and water tiles
- Can build platforms on ocean tiles (acts as land for building placement)
- Military units ignore movement penalty in shallow water

**Unique unit:** *Tidecaller* — a ranged infantry unit trained at the Castle, devastating against coastal buildings and ships, fragile in open-field combat

**Hero: Artaxerax**
A mythologized figure — half admiral, half prophet. His ability *Calima* generates an artificial fog patch on the map, hiding your units inside it for 12 seconds. Enemies walking into the cloud lose sight of your forces until they are already being attacked.

---

#### Fenicios
*"We do not need to conquer what we can simply buy."*

Carthaginian and Phoenician sailors documented the Canary Islands centuries before medieval Europe knew they existed. Hanno the Navigator's expedition logs describe islands of fire and forests. They left no settlements. They left trade routes, maps, and knowledge.

**How they play:** Fenicios are the only civilization with access to the Market from the very start of the game. They generate passive gold from idle merchant ships and can hire mercenaries — units drawn from other civilizations' rosters — directly from the Market using gold. They do not fight with their own army so much as buy everyone else's.

**Strengths:** Economy and gold generation, flexibility through mercenaries, early trade  
**Weaknesses:** No knights; no castle-tier infantry of their own; vulnerable to early aggression

**Passive bonuses:**
- Market available from Dark Age (no tech requirement)
- Merchant ships generate gold passively while docked
- Can recruit units from other civilizations' rosters at the Market (gold cost only)

**Unique unit:** *Trireme* — a fast war galley built at the Dock, with high ramming damage and bonus speed; outmaneuvers heavier ships

**Hero: Hannón el Navegante**
Based on the real Carthaginian explorer who documented the African Atlantic coast in the 5th century BC. His ability *Trade Route* activates a gold-generating corridor between two points on the map for 30 seconds — any friendly unit positioned along the route passively earns gold while it holds.

---

### Native Civilizations

The people who were already here. Each native civ occupies a specific island environment and is optimized for it. They know the terrain. They do not need roads to move fast or walls to defend well.

#### Guanches
*"Echeyde is not a mountain. It is a door. And it is ours."*

The Guanches of Tenerife lived on the slopes of the Teide volcano — *Echeyde* in their language, the sacred gateway to the underworld. They were organized into seven kingdoms (menceyatos), each led by a mencey. When the Castilians invaded, six menceys submitted. One did not.

**How they play:** Guanches are a defensive civilization built around volcanic terrain. Malpaís tiles — the black, jagged lava rock that covers much of the Canarian landscape — slow all other civilizations to a crawl. The Guanches walk through it without penalty. Their stone buildings are harder to destroy than anyone else's, and their spearmen are available from the very first age.

**Strengths:** Volcanic terrain dominance, durable buildings, strong early spear line  
**Weaknesses:** No cavalry; no gunpowder; weak at sea

**Passive bonuses:**
- No movement penalty on malpaís and lava-cooled tiles
- Stone buildings +20% HP
- Spearmen available from Dark Age without a Barracks age requirement

**Unique unit:** *Mencey's Guard* — heavy infantry trained at the Castle, high armor, strong bonus damage against cavalry. The backbone of a Guanche castle defense.

**Hero: Bencomo**
The real Mencey of Taoro. In 1494 he led the Guanche forces to a decisive victory against the Castilian army at the first Battle of Acentejo — one of the most complete defeats ever suffered by a European force at the hands of an indigenous army.

His ability *Mencey's Charge*: for 10 seconds, all friendly units within a radius gain +30% attack speed. Use it at the moment of contact, not before.

---

#### Canarii
*"The ravine is not a problem. It is a wall that builds itself."*

Gran Canaria's native people were the most politically complex of the archipelago — governed by a council of chiefs, with a warrior culture centered on individual combat. Their island's geography was their fortress: deep ravines, high plateaus, coastal cliffs.

**How they play:** Canarii have the strongest economy in the native tier. Their villagers gather food faster, their houses double as drop-off points, and their archers are cheap enough to field in large numbers early. On elevated terrain — the risco tiles found on Gran Canaria-style maps — their unique unit gains bonus attack range.

**Strengths:** Economy volume, early archer pressure, elevated terrain advantage  
**Weaknesses:** Limited heavy units; no naval strength; struggles on flat open maps

**Passive bonuses:**
- Villagers gather Food +15% faster
- Houses act as secondary drop-off points for Food
- Archers cost -20% Food

**Unique unit:** *Ravine Archer* — a light archer who gains +2 attack range when standing on a risco (cliff) tile. Devastating in defensive positions; fragile in open combat.

**Hero: Doramas**
The legendary champion of Gran Canaria. Historical chronicles describe him as a figure who could only be killed by treachery, never in fair combat. His ability *Challenge* forces a single enemy unit to attack only him for 8 seconds — separating it from its group, pulling it out of formation, isolating it for your archers.

---

#### Mahos
*"The desert does not kill you. Your hurry does."*

Lanzarote and Fuerteventura are the oldest and driest islands — flat, windswept, almost entirely without trees. The Mahos built their world out of volcanic stone because there was nothing else. Their king Guadarfía is one of the most fascinating figures of the conquest: a man who survived by being indispensable to everyone.

**How they play:** Mahos build cheap because they substitute stone for wood. Their scouts and light cavalry move faster than any other civ's equivalent units. On dune terrain they suffer no penalties and gain extended vision. They are an early-game harassment civilization — fast, unpredictable, hard to pin down.

**Strengths:** Speed, desert terrain mastery, cheap buildings, excellent scouting  
**Weaknesses:** Weak late-game; no archery depth; poor naval

**Passive bonuses:**
- Buildings cost -30% Wood (dry stone construction)
- Scouts and light cavalry +25% movement speed
- Extended line of sight on dune and desert tiles

**Unique unit:** *Sand Raider* — fast light cavalry trained at the Stable. Gains a passive speed bonus when not in active combat. Excellent for raiding villager lines and escaping before the enemy can respond.

**Hero: Guadarfía**
The Maho king who navigated the most dangerous diplomatic situation in the archipelago — allied with neither side fully, surviving the French conquest by being too useful to eliminate. His ability *Ambush* makes him invisible on dune or desert tiles until he attacks or takes damage. Combine with Sand Raiders for a classic Maho ambush.

---

### European Invaders

They did not know the terrain. They knew something else: organization, iron, and the patience of empires. Each European civ has a different reason for being in the archipelago — and a different way of fighting.

#### Francos
*"The contract was signed. The islands were already ours on paper."*

Jean de Béthencourt was not a conqueror in the traditional sense — he was a Norman lord in service to the Castilian crown, and his conquest of Lanzarote and Fuerteventura in 1402 was administrative before it was military. He exploited the Mahos' internal divisions and allied with Guadarfía. His was the first European foothold in the Atlantic.

**How they play:** Francos advance through the Ages faster and cheaper than almost any other civilization. Their cavalry is strong from Feudal Age onwards. They expand early, pressure the enemy before they are ready, and win through momentum. They are the quintessential "timing attack" civilization.

**Strengths:** Fastest age advancement, powerful cavalry, early expansion  
**Weaknesses:** Weak archery; limited naval in late game

**Passive bonuses:**
- Age advancement costs -15% resources
- Cavalry units +15% HP
- Farms constructed 20% faster

**Unique unit:** *Chevalier Normand* — medium cavalry trained at the Castle. Bonus damage against native infantry. Fast, durable, and arrives early enough to matter.

**Hero: Jean de Béthencourt**
The real Norman lord who first planted a European flag in the Atlantic archipelago. His ability *Forced Diplomacy* temporarily converts a nearby native unit to fight for you for 12 seconds — a direct reference to his historical strategy of turning native leaders into collaborators.

---

#### Britanos
*"We are not here to stay. We are here to leave with more than we arrived with."*

The English presence in the Canaries was never about colonization. It was about disruption. Francis Drake's 1595 sack of Las Palmas de Gran Canaria was the most dramatic moment of a relationship built on privateering, piracy, and commercial competition with Castile. The English came in fast, hit hard, and left.

**How they play:** Britanos have the best naval units in the game and the longest-range archers of any civilization. Their raiding economy — the Drake bonus — means that aggressive, unit-killing play actually generates resources. They reward aggression at sea and at range.

**Strengths:** Naval supremacy, long-range archery, raiding economy  
**Weaknesses:** Weak heavy cavalry upgrades; light defensive buildings

**Passive bonuses:**
- Foot archers gain +1 attack range per Age (from Feudal onwards)
- Warships attack +20% faster
- Kills near the hero generate bonus gold

**Unique unit:** *Longbowman* — foot archer with exceptional range, outranging most defensive structures at Imperial Age. The English answer to everything.

**Hero: Francis Drake**
The privateer who sacked Las Palmas in 1595 and was still repelled by the city's militia. His ability *Plunder* lasts 20 seconds — every unit killed within his radius drops extra gold for your collection. Send him into a dense fight, activate *Plunder*, and watch the economy swing.

---

#### Castellanos
*"We are not the first. We will be the last."*

The Crown of Castile funded the final and complete conquest of the archipelago. Alonso Fernández de Lugo took Tenerife in two campaigns — losing the first catastrophically at Acentejo in 1494 to Bencomo, returning in 1496 with a reinforced army to win the second. He founded La Laguna, still the historic capital of Tenerife, and completed the pacification of all seven islands.

**How they play:** Castellanos are slow to start and expensive to maintain, but their late-game power is unmatched. Every age advance unlocks a free Blacksmith technology. Their swordsmen are among the hardiest in the game. Their towers and castles have extended range. If you let Castellanos reach Imperial Age comfortably, they are very difficult to defeat.

**Strengths:** Best late-game military, superior fortifications, free technologies  
**Weaknesses:** Slow early economy; most powerful units require Castle or Imperial Age

**Passive bonuses:**
- Advancing to a new Age unlocks one free Blacksmith technology
- Swordsmen line +15% HP
- Towers and Castles have +10% attack range

**Unique unit:** *Conquistador* — armored infantry-cavalry hybrid. Bonus damage against all three native unit types. The defining unit of the late-game Castilian army.

**Hero: Don Quijote de la Mancha**
A knight errant from La Mancha — the heart of Castile, province of Toledo — who arrives in the islands convinced he is on a sacred crusade. Cervantes wrote his story in 1605, just after the Canarian conquest ended, as a satire of exactly these men: idealistic, relentless, not entirely sure what they are fighting. The windmills he famously charges at? There are real historical windmills on Lanzarote and Fuerteventura — the very islands the Castellanos set foot on first.

His active ability *Knight Errant's Charge* sends him galloping in a straight line toward a target point, damaging everything he passes through — friend or foe. Aim carefully. His passive ability *Rocinante* gives him +15% movement speed at all times, but his attacks land with a slight delay, as if he always arrives one step behind reality.

He is the strongest hero in terms of raw charge damage. He is also the one most likely to trample your own units if you use him carelessly.

## Terrain

The archipelago is not flat. Every tile type changes how units move and fight.

| Terrain | Effect | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Grass | Normal movement | Standard buildable land |
| Sand / Dune | Heavy infantry -20% speed | Mahos immune |
| Malpaís (volcanic rock) | All units -50% speed | Not buildable; Guanches immune |
| Laurisilva (dense forest) | All units -30% speed | Very high wood yield; reduces vision |
| Risco (cliff edge) | Impassable | Units adjacent gain +2 attack range |
| Shallow water | Land units -40% speed | Atlantes immune |
| Ocean | Land units blocked | Ships only; fishing available (Fishing Boats) |
| Caldera (volcanic crater) | Impassable | Controlling adjacent zone: +stone per minute |

## Resources & Economy

Four resources drive everything in *Calima*. You start with 200 Food, 75 Wood, and 50 Gold (Stone starts at 0). Normal mode uses these defaults; Abundant multiplies map deposits; Full Combat sets all four to 9999.

### The four resources

| Resource | How to gather |
|---|---|
| **Food** | Send Villagers to berry bushes, hunt animals, or build Farms. On Islands maps, Fishing Boats pull food from ocean nodes and Fish Traps provide a passive stream. |
| **Wood** | Order Villagers to chop trees. Build a Lumber Camp close to the forest — it acts as the drop-off point. |
| **Gold** | Mine gold deposits. Build a Mining Camp nearby. The Market also converts other resources to gold. |
| **Stone** | Mine stone quarries. Same drop-off logic as gold: Mining Camp required. Controlling a volcanic caldera tile provides passive stone income. |

Villagers carry up to **10 units** of any resource before returning to drop off. Build drop-off buildings close to resource nodes to reduce travel time.

### Population

You start with a cap of **15**. Every completed **House** raises the cap by **5** (no limit stated). If you hit the cap, training queues pause — nothing is built until the count drops or you build more Houses.

### Market trading

The Market lets you sell food, wood, or stone for gold, or buy resources back. Build it in Feudal Age (or earlier if you are Fenicios). Use it when one stockpile is overflowing and another is critical.

## Age Progression

Advancing Ages is the backbone of the game. Each Age unlocks new units, buildings, and techs. You advance from the Town Center once you can afford the cost.

| Age | Advance cost | Research time | What becomes available |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dark Age** | — (starting age) | — | Villager, Militia, Scout, basic buildings, Dock, Fishing Boat |
| **Feudal Age** | 500 Food | ~2 min 10 s | Archer, Heavy Scout, Blacksmith, Stable, Market, War Galley, Transport Ship |
| **Castle Age** | 800 Food + 200 Gold | ~2 min 40 s | Pikeman, Knight, University, Temple, advanced techs |
| **Imperial Age** | 1000 Food + 800 Gold | ~3 min 10 s | Wonder, Chemistry, Atonement, Blast Furnace, Plate Barding |

**Francos** advance 15% cheaper. **Castellanos** receive a free Blacksmith technology every time they advance.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Villager** | Dark | Town Center | 50 Food | Gathers all four resources; constructs buildings |
| **Scout** | Dark | Town Center (given free at start) | 80 Food | Fast explorer; weak in combat; best used for map awareness |
| **Militia** | Dark | Barracks | 60 Food + 20 Wood | Basic melee infantry; cheap and available immediately |
| **Archer** | Feudal | Barracks | 25 Wood + 45 Gold | Ranged infantry; fragile but powerful at distance |
| **Heavy Scout** | Feudal | Stable | 80 Food + 30 Gold | Fast melee cavalry; good raider; upgrades from Scout role |
| **Pikeman** | Castle | Barracks | 60 Food + 30 Gold | Heavy infantry; high armor; counters cavalry |

Heroes are not trained — each civilization starts with one, free of charge. If a hero dies, they respawn at the Town Center after a **2-minute** delay. The exception is **Regicide mode**, where hero death means instant defeat.

Three buildings handle research: **Blacksmith** (weapons and armor), **University** (advanced military and siege), and **Temple** (morale and cavalry). Each tech occupies its building for the full research time — you can only research one tech per building at a time.

Technologies apply globally to all matching units for the player who researches them. Several have prerequisites: you must complete the earlier tech before the later one becomes available.

## Victory Conditions

Three modes are available; choose one in Match Setup before the game begins.

### Conquest
Destroy **all** enemy units and buildings. The last civilization with structures standing wins. This is the default mode and the most straightforward — there is no timer, no special objective. Pressure matters, but so does patience.

### Regicide
Your **hero** is your lifeline. If your hero dies, you lose instantly — no respawn, no recovery. Your opponents share the same condition. Keep your hero alive at all costs; drive it away from the front line before a fight looks dangerous. Conversely, targeting the enemy hero is always worth the effort.

### Wonder
Build the **Wonder** (2500 Wood + 2500 Food + 2500 Stone + 5000 Gold) — available only in **Imperial Age**. Once the Wonder is complete, a **4-minute countdown** begins. If it survives those four minutes, you win.

Enemies can destroy the Wonder to cancel the countdown. If the Wonder owner's own Wonder is destroyed, they lose. The strategic dynamic is simple: once someone builds it, everyone else has four minutes to destroy it.

## Naval Units

On **Islands** maps the ocean is not just a barrier — it is a theatre of operations. Build a **Dock** to access naval units and open a second economy through fishing.

### The Dock

The Dock must be placed on land **adjacent to water**. It is the only building from which naval units can be trained (queue cap: 5). Build time is 45 seconds and costs 150 Wood.

**Hotkey:** D in the build menu.

### Unit roster

| Unit | Age | Cost | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fishing Boat** | Dark Age | 75 Wood | Economic — gathers fish from ocean nodes and returns food to the nearest Dock |
| **Transport Ship** | Feudal Age | 125 Wood | Boards military units (Militia, Archer, Pikeman, Scout, Hero) to cross the strait; Villagers cannot board |
| **War Galley** | Feudal Age | 75 Wood + 35 Gold | Ranged naval combat: 6 attack, 5.5 range, 120 HP |

### Fishing

Fish nodes appear in the ocean between islands. Right-click a fish node with a **Fishing Boat** to begin gathering. The boat gathers automatically and then returns to the nearest friendly Dock to drop off food. Right-clicking a Dock while carrying fish also triggers an immediate drop-off.

Fish counts toward the same **Food** stockpile as farms and hunting, making Fishing Boats a strong early-game investment on Islands maps — they pay for themselves quickly and do not require any land.

Fishing Boats can also build **Fish Traps** directly on ocean tiles. A Fish Trap regenerates food over time without requiring the boat to travel back and forth to a node, providing a low-maintenance passive food stream.

### Naval tips

- Build your Dock before Feudal Age on Islands maps. A War Galley in Feudal Age that the opponent cannot answer wins the strait.
- Fishing Boats are not combat units — keep War Galleys nearby to protect your fishing operation.
- Transport Ships allow you to ferry land units across the strait once the garrison system is implemented.
- **Atlantes** and **Britanos** have naval bonuses and will dominate open water if left unchecked.

## Match Setup

Before starting a skirmish you choose:

- **Map size** — Small, Medium, or Large
- **Map type** — Plains / Standard / Volcanic Coast / Desert Coast / Islands
- **Starting resources** — Scarce / Normal / Abundant / **Full Combat** (all resources 9999)
- **Civilization** — one of the eight
- **Starting Age** — Dark, Feudal, Castle, or Imperial
- **Number of Rivals** — 1 to 3 AI opponents
- **Victory Condition** — Conquest / Regicide / Wonder
- **Hero Gender** — Random / Male / Female. Each civilization has two legendary heroes (one of each gender) with different abilities; this option decides which one leads your forces.

## Tips

- **Scouts reveal terrain** — send your scout out immediately. The caldera, the laurisilva wood patches, the gold positions — knowing where they are is your first advantage.
- **Heroes respawn — except in Regicide** — if your hero dies in a normal Conquest or Wonder match, they return at the Town Center after a 2-minute delay. Do not panic, but do protect your Town Center. In Regicide mode, hero death ends your game immediately.
- **Terrain is a weapon** — if you are playing Guanches, pull enemies onto malpaís before you engage. If you are playing Mahos, fight from dune terrain where your scouts are invisible and your buildings are cheap.
- **Age timing matters** — a Francos player who reaches Feudal Age while you are still in Dark Age will hit your base before you have walls. Scout, react, adapt.
- **Research as you advance** — do not sit on an empty Blacksmith. Forging in Feudal Age gives every unit +15% attack for 75 Food. Fletching for 100 Gold doubles the value of your archers. Small investments compound fast.
- **The caldera generates stone** — if there is a volcanic caldera on the map, the team that controls the zone around it gets free stone. Do not ignore it.
- **Wonder mode is a race** — if you are winning, rush to Imperial Age and build it before your opponent can. If your opponent builds it first, you have exactly 4 minutes to destroy it. Drop everything and attack immediately.

## Visual Presentation

All of the game's art is vector and procedural (no external textures), but designed so every element is recognisable at a glance:

- **Readable units** — each unit is a clear figure for its role: the villager wears a straw hat and carries a pick, swordsmen have helmets and shields, archers draw a bow, cavalry ride horses, and each civ-unique unit has its own distinguishing features.
- **Random visual gender** — when created, each human unit is randomly male or female (50%). Women are shown with long hair. This is cosmetic only: it does not affect stats or gameplay.
- **Distinct heroines** — if your hero is female she's recognisable by long hair, a golden circlet and a gown, while keeping her weapon. Choose the hero's gender in the lobby (Random / Male / Female).
- **Team colour on buildings** — every building shows detail in its owner's colour (roofs, flags, banners, awnings, sails, domes) on top of the colour stripe at its base, so you can tell whose it is from afar.
- **Animation and orientation** — units and animals face the direction they move; deer and sheep trot as they travel.
- **Living terrain** — water has swell and coastal foam, caldera lava pulses, coasts have sandy beaches, and terrain zones blend smoothly into their neighbours.
